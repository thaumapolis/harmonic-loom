Harmonic Loom.

Two strings, one bridge. The bridge decides which partials cross — that is the instrument.

bridge passband

Two strings sharing one bridge

Each string is a real synthesised string: not one tone but a stack of partials (the fundamental plus its overtones). The two are connected by a bridge that only lets some partials across.

  • Pluck A or Pluck B, or drag on the canvas. Energy crosses the bridge and comes back — you hear it swell and fade. That is beating, not an effect.
  • Bridge centre is which partial passes. At 1 only the fundamental crosses; at 8 the fundamental is blocked, so plucking A makes B hum a note without its own root.
  • Width is how many partials around the centre get through; coupling is how fast energy crosses.
  • Stiffness is real: it pulls the higher partials sharp, so the octaves are no longer exact. That is why piano tunings are stretched.

The bars at the bottom show where the energy is — green for A, blue for B, purple for the passband. Nothing here is a recording or a canned animation.
